# X57: the EPYC's last exit-check cases: 120 and 72, and a radix choice that sees the batch

- **Status:** running
- **Owner:** x57 session, 2026-11-18
- **Phase / task:** 1 (phase 1 exit check, the tail rule); follows X47, X54

## Question

The phase 1 exit check at 71307e0 still fails on the EPYC 7763 by two cases (run
20261118-194340-02a4, band ±1.00 %: 11 of 193 c2c cases beyond noise against FFTW, at most 9
allowed): 64 × 2 backward 1.28, 1024 × 3 1.22, 1024 1.21, 1024 backward 1.18, 64 × 16 1.14,
1024 × 16 1.12, 1018 1.10, 2048 1.10, 120 1.08, 1024 × 256 1.08, 100 1.07.

(A) Are 120 and 72, which moved 1.154 → 1.221 and 1.162 → 1.250 ns per point across
separate runs since main 8c18dd2, a real regression, and from what? (B) Can the radix choice
for 1024 see the plan's data, so that 16 · 64 (X54: 0.84–0.95 of the planner's time at batches
1–256, 1.25–1.27 at 1024 × 1024 and 1.14 on the 1024² plane) is taken only where it wins?
And can the same mechanism help 2048 and 64 × 16?

## Hypothesis

(B) The two-pass plan's first pass reads 16 streams at once. While the rows and their output
stay in the L3 that is cheap; when they do not, every stream misses at once and the three-pass
plan, whose first pass reads 4, wins (X54's counters: IPC 1.66 against 2.52 at 1024 × 1024,
as FFTW's own two-pass plan). So the choice depends on the data the plan touches, not the
row: a threshold on rows + output bytes in terms of the L3 of the core's complex (CCX).

## Change under test

Arm **B** (commit 2106e44 on base 71307e0): `target_profile::fit_overrides` lists lengths
whose radices depend on the data (`avx2` double: 1024 = 16 · 64, 768 = 12 · 64, its existing
override); `chain_builder::use_row_fit`, set by `plan_core.cpp` for c2c plans of one axis,
contiguous on both sides; `machine_profile::fit_row_bytes` is the most bytes of rows plus
output (rows alone in place) for which the two-pass radices are taken; beyond it the costs'
sequence (4 · 16 · 16, 4 · 16 · 12). `set_grid_sizes` sets it to three quarters of the L3 where
a private L2 has a large L3 behind it on x86-64 (`column_team_bytes`' key, the EPYC class). No
other target has a list, so no other target's plan changes. Rows of planes and volumes keep
the rules: this is a choice for one-axis plans only.

`FIT_ROW_BYTES=0` (fbench) turns the field off in the same binary: arm **B0** is B's commit
with the old plans, so B ÷ B0 is the plan change alone, with no code placement in it.

## Method

- Screens (not deciding; DMOG, Slurm, not `--serial`): forced radices against the planner on
  catalogue `x57` (below).
- Judging, EPYC 7763 (DMOG, GCC 16.2, `avx2`, one thread), `fbench_remote.py submit --serial`,
  interleaved process rounds, A/A control:
  1. standard catalogue, filter `^c2c-[0-9]+-b`, tier `full` (the exit check's 201 cases),
     arms B0, B, FFTW 3.3.11 (MEASURE);
  2. catalogue `x57`, all its cases of 768 and 1024, arms B0, B, FFTW;
  3. tracking tier, arms H = 71307e0 and B.
- Metric: fbench's median paired ratio B ÷ B0 (B ÷ H in 3) and its interval.
- **The moved cases** are those whose plan note differs between B and B0 in the run. By the
  mechanism they can only be one-axis c2c rows of 768 and 1024 points; every other case runs
  the same plan in the same binary and does not decide (AGENTS.md section 6).
- **Decision rule, written before any judging run:**
  - (a) Over the moved cases of run 1 (expected: 1024 × 1, 3, 16 and 256 out of place, 1024
    backward, 1024 × 1 and × 3 in place), the geometric mean of B ÷ B0 is at most 0.95 and its
    interval lies below 1 − band.
  - (b) No moved case of runs 1 and 2 is slower than 1 + band (a case slower than that is
    re-run once, `--serial`, and the re-run decides; if it stands, the threshold moves below it
    or the length leaves the list before adoption).
  - (c) No plane, volume or other length moves: the notes of B and B0 agree on every case
    outside 768 and 1024 rows in runs 1 and 3.
  - (d) Tracking tier, B ÷ H: geometric mean at most 1 + band; no case past 1 + band + 2 %
    unless the case's plan is unchanged and the objects its kernels live in are byte-identical
    (placement, which does not decide).
  - Xeon and Mac are not run: their profiles list no fit overrides, so their plans cannot move.
