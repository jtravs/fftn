# X2: radix sets and orders per target

- **Status:** running (EPYC lengths up to 1024 done, planner v1 adopted; the rest pending)
- **Owner:** unassigned (written with task 1.2, 2026-11-14)
- **Phase / task:** 1.4

## Question

Which radix sequence (which radices, in which order) should the chain use for each length
on each target, and how far from the best are planner v0's rules?

## Hypothesis

Planner v0 adopts FFTL.jl's single-line rules as its starting point (DESIGN.md 11.2,
specified there and recorded in `tests/core/data/rule_radices.txt`). They were derived on
FFTL's own kernels: brute-force searches over radix multisets and orders for n ≤ 1024
(7-smooth) on the M1, checked on held-out lengths, plus interleaved A/B on Zen 2 and 3,
Tiger Lake and EPYC 9354. FFTN's passes differ (generated codelets, tails on a tile,
broadcast twiddles, copies around strided lines), so we expect the rules to be near the
best for most lengths and clearly off for some: lengths whose leading radix is below C on
wide registers (AVX2 float, AVX-512), lengths with primes above 16, and lengths where a
radix 14, 15 or 16 codelet, which the rules never choose, would remove a pass. The rules'
AVX-512 radix-16 gate (DESIGN.md 11.2) is not in v0 and is part of this question for
task 1.3's `avx512`.

## Change under test

Arm A: planner v0's rules (the commit current when the experiment starts). Arm B: for each
length, the best sequence found by the search below, forced through
`build_settings::radices` (an internal test knob, so no switch in `dev/KNOBS.toml`); the
adopted outcome is new rules, or a per-target table, that reproduce the winners.

## Method

- Cases: c2c rows, one transform and a batch of 64, every 7-smooth n ≤ 1024 and the
  standard catalogue's 1-D lengths above it, forward, double and float.
- Search: for each length, every multiset of codelet radices (2–16) whose product is n,
  in every distinct order, pruned to at most 8 passes; above 1024, the 20 best sequences of
  a cost estimate (pass count, then codelet operation counts from `src/gen/codelets.hpp`)
  plus v0's choice. Each candidate timed by fbench in one session per machine, then the
  winner and v0's choice confirmed by interleaved A/B with an A/A control.
- Machines, compilers, instruction sets, thread modes: Apple M5 Max (neon_fcma, Clang 23,
  quiet-machine check), Xeon Gold 6240 (avx2, and avx512 once task 1.3 lands; GCC 15),
  EPYC 7763 (avx2; GCC 15); one thread; scalar forced on one machine for its rules.
- Metric: per length, time(v0) ÷ time(best), the paired ratio of the confirmation A/B with
  its 95 % interval; per target, the geometric mean and the distribution over lengths.
- Decision rule, written before running: per target, replace the rule (or encode a table
  for the lengths concerned) wherever the best sequence beats v0 by more than 3 % with the
  interval clear of the A/A band; keep v0 where no candidate does. Accept new rules only if
  they reproduce at least 90 % of the measured winners and no length becomes slower than
  v0 by more than 2 % on any target measured. Rules that need cache geometry go to the cost
  model of phase 2 instead.

### Method as built

Added on 2026-11-14 before any run, to make the method above exact; the decision rule is
unchanged. The driver is [`bench/x2/search.py`](../../bench/x2/search.py) (candidates and
cost estimate in [`radices.py`](../../bench/x2/radices.py)); `search.py plan` prints the
counts and a time estimate.

- **Cases** ([`bench/catalogues/x2.toml`](../../bench/catalogues/x2.toml), generated by
  `search.py catalogue`): c2c rows, forward, out of place, double and single precision,
  batches 1 and 64. Lengths: the 133 7-smooth n ≤ 1024 that have more than one radix
  sequence (2, 3, 5, 7, 25, 49, 125, 343 and 625 have one: nothing to choose), and the 39
  standard-catalogue c2c row lengths above 1024 with more than one (not the primes, nor
  3125, 15625 and 16807, whose radix 25 or 49 is no codelet). As written above,
  non-smooth catalogue lengths below 1024 (22, 97, 121, ...) are not cases. Batch 64 only
  where 64 n ≤ 2^22 points; the ten rows from 100000 up have batch 1 only. 668 cases.
- **Candidates**: every ordered sequence of codelet radices 2–16 whose product is n, a
  prime factor above 16 being one generic pass, with at most 8 passes: 23337 over the
  smooth lengths (1911 for 960, 390 for 1024). Above 1024: the 20 best by the cost
  estimate, plus v0's choice when it is not among them (it may have more than 8 passes,
  as 2^24 does where rule 3 stops at 16384).
- **Cost estimate**: (passes, vocabulary operations per transform), compared in that
  order, ties by the sequence. Operations are Σ (n / R) × `vops` of the forward codelet of
  R in `src/gen/codelets.hpp`: the DIF variant for every pass but the last (they apply
  twiddles), the plain one for the last pass and for a leaf; a generic radix R counts
  2R² operations, plus R − 1 when twiddled. The top 20 is often the orders of one or two
  multisets.
- **v0's choice** per length is read on the machine measured, from the FFTN backend's
  case note (the plan's radices), so it is v0 for that machine's target.
- **Arms**: A is fbench's FFTN backend without options (the public API's plan); a
  candidate is the same backend with `RADICES=r1.r2...` (bench/README.md), which builds the
  plan with `build_settings::radices`. Every fbench run has A's identical copy A~.
- **Stages**, each one fbench run per length on that length's cases (up to four), all
  lengths through one stage before the next:
  1. *Screen*: every candidate except v0's own; 2 rounds of 3 batches of 1 ms after
     5 ms of warm-up, check budget 65536. Per case, the candidates are ranked by the
     median over rounds of their paired log ratio to A in the same round; one with a wrong
     output or a missing round is not ranked. A length whose estimated screening exceeds
     900 s drops the candidates last by the cost estimate until it fits, and the campaign
     file records how many (none at the default estimate: 960 is the largest, about
     330 s).
  2. *Refine*: the 4 best per case from the screen (A counts if it is among them), the
     union over the length's cases; 6 rounds of fbench's default timing (7 batches of
     2 ms, 20 ms warm-up). The winner per case has the lowest median paired ratio, and is
     v0 when no candidate is below 1. Choosing in one run and confirming in another keeps
     the confirmation free of the choice's selection bias.
  3. *Confirm*: A against the distinct winners of the length's cases, fbench's stopping
     rule (6 to 30 valid rounds, 1800 s), default timing. The per-case result is fbench's
     paired ratio of the case's winner with its 95 % interval and the run's A/A band;
     time(v0) ÷ time(best) is its reciprocal, and 1 where v0 won the refinement.
- **Report**: `search.py report --name ...` lists per case v0's sequence, the winner, the
  ratio and interval, the band, whether the winner is faster by more than 3 % with
  fbench's verdict "faster" (the decision rule's first test), and the geometric mean per
  batch and precision; run IDs per stage are in `bench-out/campaigns/x2-NAME.json`.
- **Running**: on each Slurm machine, two jobs on an exclusive node (lengths up to 1024,
  and above) through `scripts/remote/fbench_remote.py submit PROFILE fftn --campaign
  bench/x2/search.py`, with the profile's compiler; one thread (the harness is serial).
  `search.py remote PROFILE...` prints the lines. Estimated at 8 GFLOP/s in double:
  screening about 1.2 h and refine plus confirm about 2.1 h per machine (the estimate ran
  about 1.4 times long in a trial on the M5 Max). The Mac runs only when fbench's
  quiet-machine check passes.
- **Not yet possible** on the base of this experiment: fbench cannot force an instruction
  set, so each machine runs its best target (neon_fcma on the Mac, avx2 on the EPYC and,
  until task 1.3 lands, on the Xeon), and the scalar rules of "Machines" wait for that
  option.

## Results

### EPYC 7763, AVX2, lengths up to 1024

- **Where and how:** campaign `epyc-smooth`, 2026-11-14 06:21–08:15 UTC, on an exclusive
  Slurm node: AMD EPYC 7763 64-Core Processor (128 logical CPUs, 32 MiB L3 per the
  harness), Linux 5.14 (el9); GCC 16.2.0, Release (`-O3 -DNDEBUG`), so not the GCC 15 of
  "Machines"; instruction set `avx2` (fbench's FFTN backend, the best target); one thread.
  Load: 1-minute load average at the start of each round 0.23 to 1.33 (median 0.74) on 128
  CPUs. Source: commit f8d9c7f (the harness's build of each arm). All 133 lengths ran
  every stage: no candidate dropped, none untimed.
- **Runs:** screening 20261114-062126-1dae to 20261114-072134-6c50 (133 runs), refinement
  20261114-072235-f4d0 to 20261114-075047-e2da (133), confirmation 20261114-075105-41a2 to
  20261114-081505-3863 (120: in 13 lengths v0 won every case's refinement);
  `bench-out/campaigns/x2-epyc-smooth.json` maps each length to its three runs.
  `search.py report --name epyc-smooth` gives the per-case table.
- **v0 against the best:** geometric mean of time(v0) ÷ time(winner) over the 133 lengths
  1.257 (batch 1, double), 1.442 (batch 1, float), 1.246 (batch 64, double), 1.434
  (batch 64, float). The decision rule's first test (winner faster by more than 3 % with
  fbench's verdict "faster", interval clear of the A/A band) holds for 101, 102, 103 and
  105 of the 133 cases of each group; v0 won the refinement in 24 to 27 of them (9 in each
  group are the lengths up to 16, where v0's leaf won every case). The confirmation's A/A band was 1.2 %
  at the median, 3.0 % at the 90th percentile.
- **Ties:** in 83 of the 248 double cases and 71 of the 248 float cases above 16 the
  refinement's best two candidates are within the confirmation's band of each other.
  Batches agree: the batch-1 and batch-64 winners are the same sequence in 98 (double) and
  103 (float) of the 124 lengths above 16, and one is within the other's band in 118 and
  121.
- **Patterns:** the winners have fewer passes than v0 (2.7 on average against 3.6) and use
  the radices 10, 12, 14, 15 and 16 that v0 never chooses (1024: 4.16.16 or 8.8.16; 945:
  15.7.9; 1000: 10.10.10 in double). Tails decide the rest: wherever C divides n the
  winner's first radix is a multiple of C in 194 of 196 double cases and 139 of 146 float
  cases, and float prefers a first radix of exactly 4 even at the cost of a pass (100:
  4.5.5 in 0.57 of the time of 10.10; 2.10.5 takes 3.6 times as long as 4.5.5).
- **Rules tried** (reproduction as counted below, in sample): v0 20.5 %; fewest passes
  with a first radix that is a multiple of C, ties by a padded operation count, 72 %; the
  sequence of lowest padded operation count with fitted per-pass and per-tail costs, 78 %
  (79 % with costs per unit proportional to the codelets' operation counts); a table of
  costs per pass kind and radix with tails proportional to the unit cost, 83 %; with a
  free cost per tail and radix, the model adopted, 93.8 % before overrides. One table for
  both precisions reached 90.8 %, so each C has its own.
- **The fit:** the model of DESIGN.md 11.2 rule 4 plus a constant per transform, per
  precision, batches 1 and 64 together. Data: every candidate's median paired log ratio to
  v0 from the refinement (weight 1) and, for candidates not refined, from the screening
  (weight 0.3); 7-smooth lengths 18 to 1024. Least squares on log(cost(candidate)) −
  log(cost(v0)) − observed, soft-L1 loss with scale 0.05 (SciPy's `least_squares`), costs
  per unit bounded below by 0.4 × (codelet vocabulary operations + 2R), the middle
  radix-4 unit fixed at (operations + 2R) to set the scale, then multiplied by 10 and
  rounded to integers; residual 3.5 % (double) and 3.9 % (float) RMS, 2.8 % and 2.9 % on
  the refined candidates. The fit was made with SciPy; it is now
  [`bench/x2/fit.py`](../../bench/x2/fit.py) (standard library), which on this campaign
  reaches the same objective, gives the float table and the six overrides exactly and a
  double table with the same choice at every length (some double tail costs are not
  determined by the data). Two-fold cross-validation over the lengths (fit
  on half, count on the other half, before overrides): 92 %.
- **Counting reproduction:** a case is reproduced when v1's sequence is the refinement's
  winner, or when its median paired log ratio in the same refinement run is within the
  confirmation run's A/A band (fbench's band, at least 1 %) of the winner's; where v0 won
  and there was no confirmation, the band is 1 %. A leaf (n ≤ 16) is the only sequence
  measured besides its splits and counts as reproduced when it won. Where v1's sequence was
  not refined the screening run would have been used; it never was needed: every v1
  sequence was among the refined candidates.
- **Planner v1 against the measurements:** 509 of 532 cases reproduced (95.7 %; 473 of the
  496 cases above 16, 95.4 %): 419 exact, 54 within the band, 36 leaves. Per group: 124,
  129, 128 and 128 of 133 (batch 1 double, batch 1 float, batch 64 double, batch 64 float).
  The 23 misses are 1.3 % to 4.2 % behind the winner, all in the refinement (worst: 28,
  batch 64 float, 4.7 against 7.4). Without overrides the costs' choice was more than 2 %
  slower than v0 in six cases (42 batch 1 double, 9.5 %; 70 batch 1 double; 140 batch 64
  float; 350 batch 64 double; 490 batch 1 double; 700 batch 1 float), all from the order
  of a radix 7; the six overrides are those lengths' winners, which no batch of the same
  precision measured slower than v0. With them no case is slower than v0 by more than
  2 %: the worst are 882 batch 64 double (14.7.9, 1.1 % slower), 686 and 63 batch 64 double
  (0.8 %). Estimated from the refinement, the geometric mean of time(v0) ÷ time(v1) is
  1.255, 1.444, 1.245 and 1.431 for the four groups, against the winners' 1.257, 1.442,
  1.246 and 1.434 in the confirmation.

### Apple M5 Max, `neon_fcma`, lengths up to 1024

Campaign mac-smooth (Release, Clang 23, the planner then being v0; the Mac loaded by other
work throughout, so fbench's interleaving and A/A band carried the noise). Planner v0
against the best sequence, geometric means over 133 lengths: 1.066 (b1, double), 1.113
(b1, float), 1.073 (b64, double), 1.119 (b64, float); far closer than on the EPYC, but up
to 1.6 at single lengths. `bench/x2/fit.py --isa neon_fcma` fitted tables for C = 1 and 2:
within 2 % of the measured winner in 81.5 % (double) and 83.1 % (float) of cases, against
48.4 % and 64.5 % for the AVX2 tables NEON used under planner v1. A/B of the two tables
(run 20261114-101523-3d56, 496 cases n = 17–1024, band ±1.67 %, load median 6.4): NEON
tables 0.964 times the time (geometric mean), 124 faster, 363 no evidence, 9 slower; the
three worst (490, 686, 882 in double, 1.25–1.40) keep the AVX2 tables' choices as overrides.
Adopted.

## Verdict

For AVX2 (EPYC 7763, lengths up to 1024): planner v0 is replaced. v0 was more than 3 %
slower than the best sequence, clear of the band, in about 77 % of the cases (1.25 times
slower in double and 1.44 in float, geometric means). Planner v1 (DESIGN.md 11.2) meets
the acceptance rule there: 95.7 % of the measured winners reproduced and no case slower
than v0 by more than 2 %. Adopted as the default for every target. On NEON (Apple M5 Max) its own fitted tables
replaced the AVX2 ones (3.6 % faster, see Results). On AVX-512,
x86-64-v2, AVX and scalar, and for lengths above 1024, it applies by analogy (the table
of the nearest C) and is unmeasured until the campaigns under "Pending" run. Task 1.3
decides AVX-512's table, including FFTL's radix-16 rule for it.
